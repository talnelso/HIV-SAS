Short Description: The Indianapolis TGA partnered with academia to create a program leveraging HIV surveillance data to shift from retrospective to proactive identification of those at risk of falling out of HIV care. Suggested Citation: McCaskill TL, Butler MI, Gumns T, Wiehe SE. Leveraging HIV surveillance data to improve retention in care. Presented at the 2026 National Ryan White Conference on HIV Care & Treatment; Aug. 7, 2026; Washington, D.C.

Full Abstract:

The Marion County Public Health Department (MCPHD) conducts linkage and engagement across the 10‐county Ryan White Part A Indianapolis TGA. Their work reengages people living with HIV (PLWH) who are lost to follow up back to the system of care. Historically, these services were provided retrospectively. Our objective was to leverage the same HIV surveillance data for predictive alerts, enabling a proactive approach.

MCPHD worked with the Indiana University School of Medicine to develop a program that utilizes HIV surveillance data and an algorithm to generate alerts to identify PLWH who are within 30-60 days of falling out of a retained in care status. We tested and refined the algorithm to decrease unwarranted alerts (e.g., deceased or relocated PLWH) and to differentiate alert timing based on if the most recent viral load was detectable (≥50 copies/mL) or undetectable. Testing indicates this program will result in relinkage efforts that begin 106 days (IQR 52-190) earlier than the current retrospective method. MCPHD is implementing and evaluating the program now. Results will be available at the time of the conference.

Routine medical care is critical to the health of PLWH and to ending the HIV epidemic. Our program shifts from retrospective methods to proactive identification of PLWH at risk of falling out of care. Our code is freely available for use by others or can be adapted to Ryan White’s CareWARE database or electronic medical records so that outreach staff can proactively identify care attrition.
